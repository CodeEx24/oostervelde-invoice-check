# Task audit: invoice checking, Woonkring Oostervelde finance

_Transcript of a call between Sanne Verbeek (accounts payable, Woonkring Oostervelde) and an MSTR consultant, 3 February 2026. Translated from Dutch and lightly cleaned up. About 20 minutes._

**MSTR:** Thanks for making time. Can you walk me through what you do when the contractor invoices come in?

**Sanne:** Sure. At the start of the month I get everything from the previous month. About forty invoices, from our four maintenance contractors. Hoekstra does heating and plumbing, De Wit is the painter, Bosman does roofs, and Jansen is electrical. Every one of them has their own layout, of course. Hoekstra's are neat, De Wit writes them like a letter, honestly.

**MSTR:** And what do you check?

**Sanne:** Whether they charge what we agreed. We have a framework contract with a price list. So I take every line and look it up in the price list. That's the bulk of it, the prices.

**MSTR:** How long does that take?

**Sanne:** Two days, roughly. Sometimes more, if there's something weird.

**MSTR:** What counts as weird?

**Sanne:** Hours, mostly. Every work order has a maximum number of hours. If they go over it, they need an approval from us, and that's in the work order. So then I have to open the work order export, find the order, check the approval. That export is a nightmare, it's one big file. To be honest, I don't always open it. If the amount is small I let it go.

**MSTR:** What about the call-out fees? I saw those in the contract.

**Sanne:** Yes, voorrijkosten. They're only allowed on emergency jobs. I check those when I remember. Hoekstra especially likes to sneak them in on regular work.

**MSTR:** And the price increase this year?

**Sanne:** Right, everything went up 3.2 percent on the first of January. So invoices from January use the new prices. That one is easy, I just check the invoice is from this year.

**MSTR:** Okay. And VAT?

**Sanne:** 21 percent. Well, except the painters, there's something with 9 percent for labor. For older homes, I think. I usually just check that the painter has two VAT lines.

**MSTR:** Has anything gone wrong in the past?

**Sanne:** Last year we paid an invoice twice. Same job, Jansen sent it again a month later with a new number, and nobody noticed. That was almost two thousand euros. It came out at the year-end audit, which was not a fun conversation.

**MSTR:** How did that happen?

**Sanne:** Because I look at invoices one by one. I don't compare them to each other. You can't, with forty of them, in my head.

**MSTR:** What happens when you find something?

**Sanne:** I email the contractor. That's actually the part I hate. I have to explain exactly which line, what the contract says, what the amount should be. They always push back, so it has to be airtight. If I write "your price is wrong" they just say "no it isn't". I need the line, the rule, and the number. Writing those emails takes me longer than finding the problem, sometimes.

**MSTR:** So if you had to pick one thing to get rid of?

**Sanne:** The looking up. No, wait. The emails. Hm. Both? Honestly, if something gave me a list of what's wrong, with the reason and the amount, ready to send, I'd be very happy. And I'd want to see why it thinks something is wrong, because I'm the one who signs off. I'm not going to approve forty invoices on a computer's word.

**MSTR:** Fair. And credit notes?

**Sanne:** Those come in sometimes when a contractor admits a mistake. They refer to the original invoice. Then I just net them out.

**MSTR:** Anything else I should know?

**Sanne:** Invoices with a work order number I don't recognize. That happens. Then I can't check anything, so it goes on hold until the housing officer confirms the job. Oh, and one more thing. Extra work, meerwerk. Up to 250 euros they can just do it. Above that they need our approval first. I think if they don't have it, we only pay the 250? I'd have to check the contract.

**MSTR:** We'll check it. Last question. I'd like to compare against what you did last month. Can I have your verdicts on a few invoices?

**Sanne:** Sure, I'll send you the first ten from January, with my notes. They're in Dutch, sorry.
